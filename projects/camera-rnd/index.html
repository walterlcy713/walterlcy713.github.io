The question

I independently developed and integrated camera-driven interaction for two Unreal prototypes. I built the physics scene myself; an art-team colleague supplied the water effect for the Niagara prototype.

Seeing the room

Python sends person-position and depth data from the OAK camera to Unreal. I use that data to create and update virtual objects that drive the interaction.

People as physics

Unreal creates an object for each detected person, tracks it by ID and updates its position from camera data. Each object has collision for interaction with the scene; when the person leaves the camera view, Unreal removes the object.

Water interaction

I connected the camera-driven collision objects to a Niagara water effect supplied by the art team, implementing the integration and interaction.

Where it went

In testing, camera accuracy and update rate were lower than I expected, limiting the quality of the interaction.

The later OAK systems for Studio City’s Boxing/Racing pillars and movie-trailer effects extended these experiments. See the attraction case for those systems and the separate ZED main-gameplay input.
